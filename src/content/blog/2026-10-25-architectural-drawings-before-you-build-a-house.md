Many building problems start long before the first brick is laid. They start when construction begins without clear drawings. Architectural drawings turn your ideas into a plan that the owner, the designer and the builder can all follow.

## What architectural drawings actually are

Architectural drawings show how a building will look and how its spaces will work. A typical set for a house or apartment building includes:

- **Site plan:** where the building sits on the plot, with setbacks and access.
- **Floor plans:** the layout of every floor, with rooms, doors, windows and stairs.
- **Elevations:** how each side of the building looks from outside.
- **Sections:** a cut through the building showing heights, floor levels and roof.
- **Details:** doors, windows, stairs, kitchens, bathrooms and other items that need extra information.

## Why finish the drawings first

Changing a line on paper costs almost nothing. Changing a wall that is already built costs materials, labour and time. Complete drawings help you to:

1. **Agree the layout early.** Walk through every room on paper before you commit.
2. **Get an accurate quotation.** Contractors can only price what they can see. Clear drawings make quotations easier to compare.
3. **Reduce changes on site.** Fewer surprises means fewer delays and fewer arguments.
4. **Coordinate other work.** Structure, electrical and plumbing all depend on the architectural layout.

## Questions to settle before drawings start

Think about these points and discuss them with your designer:

- How many people will live in the building, and how might that change in the future?
- Do you want to add another floor later? If so, tell your designer now so the structure can allow for it.
- How should the building handle sunlight, ventilation and privacy?
- What is your realistic budget? A design that ignores budget often has to be redrawn.

## Drawings and approvals

Local authorities usually have their own rules for plans, setbacks and permissions. Requirements differ by area and can change, so confirm the current rules with the relevant authority before you finalise the design.
